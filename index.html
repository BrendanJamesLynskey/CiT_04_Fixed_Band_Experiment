Compute in Transit — Presentation 04

The Fixed-Band Experiment

A link module that computes in transit cannot do DeMo’s top-k: it has no comparisons. Could a fixed frequency band, which needs none, take top-k’s place? A 15.8M-parameter GPT trained on TinyStories with eight simulated workers says no. This deck shows the loss and the bytes, why the band misses the momentum’s energy, how the emulated optics first diverged and how it was fixed, and what that means for a link module.

01

The Question

DeMo (Peng et al., arXiv:2411.19870, ICLR 2026) lets each worker keep its own momentum. Every step it takes a chunked DCT of that momentum, sends the top-k coefficients of each chunk, and subtracts what it sent; the workers all-gather the values, and each applies the sign of the averaged update.

Top-k, as published

  • Keeps the largest coefficients of each chunk: the most energy for the bytes.
  • Needs comparisons to find them, and sends their positions as well as their values.
  • Not in the module’s published operation set.

A fixed band

  • Keeps the same positions in every chunk, every step: a gather, no comparisons.
  • Sends values only, so half the bytes of top-k at the same k.
  • Fits the module, but keeps the energy only if the momentum’s energy sits in that band.
Two questions

1. Does DeMo still converge, per byte sent, when top-k is replaced by a fixed band? 2. If the band is computed by an analogue optical transform, with its quantisation, clipping and readout noise, how much precision does it need?

The Setup

A GPT of 15,833,472 parameters (8 layers, width 384, context 256), trained on TinyStories with a 4,096-token byte-level BPE. Eight simulated workers in one process, each with its own data shard, momentum and DeMo state; 8 sequences per worker per step; 3,000 steps. DeMo’s momentum decay is 0.999, and every compressed variant keeps 1/16 of the coefficients.

Bytes per Step: Payload and Wire (Interactive)

Two ways to count. Payload is what a worker originates each step. Wire is what it actually sends in its ring collective: an all-reduce for AdamW, an all-gather for DeMo, because each worker’s top-k positions differ.

Source: results.md, section 1. Values in BF16; top-k positions at the bytes needed for a position within a chunk (with int64 positions, as the reference code sends them, top-k is 9,895,920 bytes, 2.5× the compact count). Eight workers.

On a ring of N workers an all-reduce sends 2(N−1)/N times its buffer, close to twice whatever N is; an all-gather sends (N−1) times the payload. So with eight workers the payload ratios of 8× and 16× become 2.0× and 4.0× on the wire, and they shrink further as workers are added.

Why: the Band Misses the Momentum's Energy

A fixed band can only work if the energy of what DeMo compresses sits in it. The diagnostics measure the share of energy each selector would keep at the same k, on worker 0, averaged over every diagnostic after step 200, all tensors together. A random set keeps about the keep fraction (1/16, about 0.06).

Source: results.md, section 2. Momentum = a plain momentum of the same decay, never compressed; delta = what DeMo actually compresses (momentum plus the residual error feedback keeps); gradient = the step’s gradient.

  • In DeMo’s 2-D chunks the low band keeps 0.013 of the momentum’s energy: about a fifth of a random set (0.065), against 0.643 for top-k at the same k.
  • The gradient’s low band is much fuller (0.305 in 2-D). But DeMo compresses a momentum (decay 0.999), not a gradient, and in 2-D chunks the momentum’s low band is nearly empty.
  • 1-D chunks help the band, but not enough: 0.146 of the momentum, about a third of top-k’s 0.429.
  • The delta holds nothing in the band: error feedback removes the band every step, so what remains to compress is all out of band. That residual becomes the optics’ problem (slide 08).
07

Emulating the Optics

Variant E computes the 1-D 256 band as an analogue optical transform would, so that its errors enter training. The emulator follows the module’s constraints: power-of-2 transform sizes, binary (NRZ) input, and only multiply-accumulates for anything data-dependent, so no per-chunk maximum.

delta (BF16) → scale to full scale, saturate → 8-bit two’s complement → one optical pass per bit plane, noisy readout → recombine with weights 2b
  • Input full scale: kappa = 4 times the delta’s RMS, measured over a calibration window (steps 200 to 299): a sum of squares, no comparisons. Inputs beyond it saturate, and the clip rate is logged.
  • Bit planes: each binary digit of the input passes through the transform separately; the planes are recombined with weights 2b, the top one negative. The number of planes is the number of optical passes (4, 8 or 12 tested).
  • Readout noise per plane: RMS = FS × 2−ENOB × 2/√12, with FS = 16, the largest row L1 norm of a 256-point orthonormal DCT. ENOB 6, 8, 10 and 12.
  • The emulator is accurate on white input (where the band holds 1/16 of the energy): 8 planes without readout noise reproduce the exact band to 0.9% RMS error, 12 planes to 0.2%, and 8 planes at ENOB 8 to 16%.
  • A perfect-optics control (no quantisation, no noise) is C with E’s power-of-2 chunking: at width 384 the 17 LayerNorm vectors are cut into runs of 128 rather than DeMo’s 192.

Source: docs/optics_investigation.md, sections 1, 2 and 5; results.md, section 3. The noise model is the series’ own, not a measured characteristic of the module.

The First Optics Runs Diverged

As first designed, error feedback removed the exact band from each sender’s delta, while every receiver applied the optics’ version. All six runs diverged; the same band computed exactly trained normally.

  • The full scale goes stale: set at step 300 by a residual that keeps growing, it saturates 30 to 50% of inputs.
  • The band sinks below the error: per band coefficient the RMS is about √(16 × share) of the input RMS, 0.016 by the end. Quantisation alone adds 0.009; readout noise at ENOB 8 adds 0.17 after recombining 8 planes (0.011 at ENOB 12).
  • The error is a bias, not noise: the residual changes slowly, so quantisation and clipping errors repeat almost identically each step, are never corrected, and DeMo’s sign update turns a consistent error into a full step the wrong way, every step.

The Fix: Feed Back What Was Sent

Each idea was first tried on a small model on the CPU (TinyShakespeare, 4 layers, width 128, 600 steps, 1-D 256 chunks; about 12 minutes a run) before spending GPU time. Final validation loss at step 600, with the clip rate over the last interval:

Configuration (8 planes, ENOB 8 unless stated)Final loss
C, exact band (reference)2.464
E as first designed: full scale fixed, feedback of the exact band8.809 (41.8%)
Full scale re-set every 50 steps, feedback of the exact band3.404 (0.6%)
… at ENOB 123.069 (0.7%)
… with no readout noise at all2.967 (0.7%)
Full scale re-set every 50 steps, feedback of the band sent2.535 (0.5%)
Full scale fixed, feedback of the band sent2.520 (5.7%)
  1. The failure reproduces at small scale.
  2. Re-setting the full scale removes most clipping, yet the loss still drifts: saturation was not the whole problem.
  3. Even with no readout noise the loss stays 0.5 above the exact band: quantisation and clipping errors, never fed back, are enough.
  4. Feeding back what was actually sent fixes it, with or without re-setting the full scale.
Why the fix is physically allowed

DeMo exchanges its compressed values with an all-gather: every worker receives every worker’s values, its own included. So a sender sees what the optics produced for its own data, noise and all, and can subtract that from its delta instead of the exact band. Nothing extra crosses the link. The optional full-scale re-set uses the delta’s mean square over an interval: multiply-accumulates only.

Source: docs/optics_investigation.md, sections 3 and 4; results/shakespeare-optics-check/results.md.

  • Every repaired run trains, with clipping near 0.3%. Re-setting the full scale adds nothing once errors are fed back: 3.017 with it, 3.021 without.
  • ENOB 8 is enough for the band itself. ENOB 6 costs 0.3 against the exact band (3.423). Four planes cost 0.12 against eight; twelve gain nothing.
  • With 8 or more planes, from ENOB 8 up, the optics band ends below the exact band, by up to 0.25 (ENOB 10). The control (3.141, 0.017 above C’s mean) rules out the power-of-2 chunking as the cause; it ran on a T4, the others on an A100.
A hypothesis, not a result

Why would an inexact band beat the exact one? Possibly because quantisation and clipping are non-linear: the values the optics produces carry a little of the large out-of-band residual into the 16 positions sent, and with feedback of what was sent that part is then removed from the delta, so some out-of-band information reaches the model. It would fit the random band beating the low band. But every optics run here is a single seed, and on TinyShakespeare the repaired optics ended slightly above the exact band (2.520 to 2.535 against 2.464). Testing it needs several seeds and a measurement of the out-of-band energy in the values sent.

What It Means for a Link Module

The experiment answers deck 02’s question for this model and scale. Against the five tests of deck 01:

TestFixed band computed in transit
Operationspass A transform and a gather; full-scale tracking by multiply-accumulate. Top-k would need comparisons.
Precisionpass ENOB 8 and 8 planes match or beat the exact band, once the values sent are fed back.
Bottleneck (convergence per byte)fail Every fixed band ends more than a nat above top-k; top-k reaches the band’s final loss in 400 steps.
Placementtension See below.

What a fixed band could gain

Every worker sends the same positions, and DeMo averages before taking the sign, so a fixed band could be summed in the network rather than gathered: an all-reduce of 1,979,184 bytes sends 3.46 MB per worker with eight workers, 16× under dense on the wire too (derived from results.md’s payload with the ring formula; not a run). That is the strongest case for computing in transit.

What the fix depends on

Feedback of the values sent relies on the all-gather returning each worker’s own optical output. An all-reduce returns only the sum. Keeping the fix would need the module to hand each sender back its own result (a loopback), or the band to be computed at the sender. And summing streams needs them to meet: a switch, not a pluggable.

Verdict

A photonic link module (arXiv:2608.21536) cannot do DeMo’s top-k, and a fixed band computed in transit is not a substitute for it at this scale: the optics is precise enough, but the band is the wrong selection. Limits, Next Steps, and What to Take Away

Limits

  • One small model (15.8M parameters), one data set, 3,000 steps, eight simulated workers.
  • One seed for every E and F run; the optics-beats-exact finding rests on single seeds.
  • The learning rate was tuned on top-k and AdamW only, and reused for every fixed-band variant.
  • The optics is emulated with the series’ own noise model, not measured on the module.
  • Wire bytes assume ring collectives; no latency or link model yet.

Next

  • A discrete-event model of training transit across data centres, fed by these bytes per sync (wire, not payload) and the steps-to-loss ladder.
  • More seeds for E, and a measurement of out-of-band energy in the values sent, to test the hypothesis of slide 10.
  • Selections without comparisons that track the momentum: a band re-calibrated periodically, or fixed random sets that change by a known schedule.
  • A fixed band does not replace top-k. Top-k ends at 1.785, AdamW at 1.646, every low-frequency band 1.24 to 1.37 above top-k, a random fixed set 0.92 above.
  • Half the bytes does not pay: top-k reaches 3.124 in 400 steps (1.58 GB); the band needs 2,667 steps and 5.28 GB to reach 3.158.
  • Why: in 2-D chunks the low band keeps 0.013 of the momentum’s energy, a fifth of a random set; top-k keeps 0.643.
  • Count wire bytes: with eight workers DeMo’s all-gather turns 8× and 16× payload savings into 2.0× and 4.0×.
  • The optics needs its errors fed back. Feeding back the exact band diverged in all six runs; feeding back the values sent, which an all-gather returns, trains, and ENOB 8 suffices.
  • The optics improvement over the exact band is a hypothesis, from one seed per run.
